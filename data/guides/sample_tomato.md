## Raising tomato seedlings

Tomatoes are usually started in a nursery and transplanted. Sow seeds in a clean
nursery bed or trays and transplant seedlings about 3 to 4 weeks later, when they
have 4 to 6 true leaves. Transplant in the cool of the late afternoon to reduce
transplant shock, and water immediately.

## Watering tomatoes

Tomatoes need steady, even moisture. Irregular watering — letting the soil dry
out and then flooding it — is a common cause of blossom-end rot, where the bottom
of the fruit turns black and leathery. Water regularly and mulch to keep soil
moisture even.

## Common tomato disease: Early blight

Early blight shows up as dark brown spots with concentric rings, like a target,
usually starting on the older, lower leaves. Remove and destroy affected leaves,
avoid wetting the foliage when watering, and rotate tomatoes with non-related
crops so the disease does not build up in the soil.

## Staking

Staking or trellising tomato plants keeps the fruit off the ground, improves air
flow, and reduces fruit rot and disease. Tie the main stem loosely to the stake
as the plant grows.
