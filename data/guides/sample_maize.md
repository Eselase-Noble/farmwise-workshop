## When to plant maize

In the major rainy season, plant maize at the onset of steady rains, typically
when the soil is moist to a depth of several centimetres. Planting into dry soil
risks poor germination. A common spacing is about 75 cm between rows and 40 cm
between plants, with two seeds per hole thinned to one healthy seedling.

## Fertilizer for maize

Maize responds strongly to nitrogen. A common practice is to apply a compound
fertilizer (such as NPK 15-15-15) at planting, followed by a top-dressing of a
nitrogen fertilizer (such as urea) about 4 to 6 weeks after germination when the
plants are knee-high. Always apply fertilizer to moist soil and avoid placing it
directly on the seed.

## Weed control

The first 6 weeks are the critical weed-free period for maize. Weeds that grow
during this window compete heavily for nitrogen and water and can sharply reduce
yield. Weed early, either by hand or with an appropriate herbicide.

## Common maize problem: Fall armyworm

Fall armyworm larvae feed inside the leaf whorl and leave ragged holes and moist
sawdust-like droppings. Scout fields early in the morning. Where infestation is
light, hand-picking and destroying egg masses can help. Act early, because older
larvae hidden deep in the whorl are much harder to control.
