## Why soil pH matters

Soil pH controls how easily plants can take up nutrients. Most food crops grow
best in slightly acidic to neutral soil, roughly pH 6.0 to 7.0. When soil is too
acidic (low pH) or too alkaline (high pH), nutrients such as phosphorus can
become locked up and unavailable even if they are present in the soil.

## Correcting acidic soil

If a soil test shows the soil is too acidic (pH well below 6), agricultural lime
can be applied to raise the pH over time. Lime acts slowly, so it is usually
applied and worked into the soil before the planting season rather than
expecting an immediate effect.

## Building organic matter

Adding well-rotted compost or farmyard manure improves soil structure, helps the
soil hold water, and feeds soil life. Organic matter also acts as a buffer,
making the soil less prone to sudden swings in pH and moisture.

## Crop rotation

Growing the same crop in the same field season after season drains specific
nutrients and lets pests and diseases build up. Rotating crops — for example,
following a heavy feeder like maize with a legume — helps restore soil nitrogen
and breaks pest cycles.
